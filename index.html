I.

Project Context & Scientific Background

This doctoral position sits within Project AMISTAD (Adaptive Mechanisms Influencing Socializing and Time Alone in Daily-life), a research initiative funded under the PIUNA Strategy 25–30 at the University of Navarra. AMISTAD investigates how the quality and balance of everyday social interaction and solitude shape individuals' experience of loneliness in contemporary, digitalized societies.

Loneliness has become a pressing public health concern, with nearly one in four Spanish adults reporting frequent loneliness. Paradoxically, this rise coincides with an unprecedented expansion of opportunities for social connection. AMISTAD addresses this paradox by shifting focus from the quantity to the quality and contextual fit of everyday social and solitude experiences, examining how digital media, physical environments, and individual differences moderate these dynamics.

"Loneliness does not primarily stem from insufficient social contact, but from a mismatch between an individual's social needs and how they experience and balance social interaction and solitude in everyday life."

The project employs a rigorous multi-wave experience sampling methodology (ESM) combined with passive smartphone sensing, collecting over 70,000 real-time reports from nationally representative adult samples across Spain. It brings together expertise in interpersonal communication, social psychology, neuroarchitecture, and film aesthetics.

II.

Doctoral Specialisation: Teachers as a Study Population

This specific doctoral project adapts the AMISTAD framework to focus on schoolteachers as its primary study population — a group of exceptional theoretical and societal relevance. Teaching is among the most emotionally and psychologically demanding professions: teachers invest considerable social and emotional energy in their daily interactions with students, colleagues, and families, while simultaneously being required to maintain high levels of psychological stability and resilience.

This combination makes teachers an ideal population through which to examine the dynamics of the social-solitude balance. Their professional context creates unique pressures on everyday social experience — high-frequency, emotionally charged interactions, limited opportunities for restorative solitude during working hours, and the increasing intrusion of digital communication across professional and personal boundaries.

  • Teachers experience structurally high social demands, making the social-solitude balance particularly salient and consequential for their well-being.
  • The school environment provides a rich context for examining how physical spaces (classrooms, shared areas) mediate social experience and solitude quality.
  • Digital connectivity — e-mail, messaging apps, learning platforms — continuously reshapes the boundaries between professional interaction and private recovery time.
  • Findings will yield directly applicable insights for educational policy, workplace well-being programmes, and loneliness prevention strategies.
III.

Research Objectives & Work Plan

The dissertation will address four principal research objectives, mirroring the broader AMISTAD structure while situating all inquiry within the professional and personal life of teachers:

WP 1
Social–Solitude Balance

Modelling the frequency, quality, and transitions between social interaction and solitude in teachers' daily lives, and their effect on momentary and cumulative loneliness.

WP 2
Digital Media Use

Examining how smartphone and digital platform use moderates social and solitude experiences, their quality, fit, and balance in a teaching context.

WP 3
Physical Environments

Investigating how school and home spatial conditions (lighting, acoustics, biophilic elements) shape opportunities for restorative solitude and quality social engagement.

WP 4
Individual Differences

Exploring how career stage, household composition, and personal characteristics moderate the social-solitude balance and susceptibility to loneliness among teachers.

The methodology will employ the same experience sampling procedure as AMISTAD, adapted for teacher samples: a 15-minute baseline survey followed by a two-week ESM period with five semi-random daily prompts, passive smartphone log data, and end-of-day surveys. The doctoral student will have direct access to established partnerships with Netquest (fieldwork) and Avicenna (ESM software), as well as the broader AMISTAD data infrastructure.

IV.

Expected Academic Outputs

The dissertation will comprise a minimum of three peer-reviewed empirical articles, submitted to high-impact journals across education, social psychology, and interpersonal communication. The following outlets are envisaged as primary submission targets, in line with the scientific quality standards already established by the research group:

  • Journal of Educational Psychology or Teaching and Teacher Education — for findings specific to the teaching population and their professional well-being.
  • Journal of Personality and Social Psychology or Social Psychological and Personality Science — for contributions to the broader social-solitude balance literature.
  • Human Communication Research or Journal of Communication — for work on digital media moderation and interpersonal dynamics.

All articles will be pre-registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF) prior to data collection. Data and analysis code will be made publicly available upon publication, in keeping with the open science principles of the AMISTAD project. Results will be presented at leading international conferences, including the International Communication Association (ICA) and the Society for Personality and Social Psychology (SPSP), as well as education-focused conferences as appropriate.
